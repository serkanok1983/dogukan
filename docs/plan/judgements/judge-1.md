# Judge 1 of 3 — the six-year-old's seat, and the game designer's

Role: I judge primarily as Doğukan (6, emerging reader, phone in hand, Super Bear Adventure and Minecraft one tap away) and secondarily as a mobile game designer. The questions I hold every proposal against:

1. Will he open *this* instead of Super Bear / Minecraft / shorts?
2. Is it fun in the first 60 seconds, with zero reading?
3. Is there a reason to come back tomorrow?
4. Is the first-week loop believable, or does it collapse into "homework before play" by day 2?

Inputs read in full: `proposals/game-designer.md`, `proposals/pedagogy.md`, `proposals/mobile-production.md`, `proposals/parent-wellbeing.md`; maps `shell-ui-mobile.md`, `game-infra.md`, `learning-content.md`, `encyclopedia.md`, `production-readiness.md`; spot checks of `src/hooks/useGameScore.ts`, `src/activities/games/superBearLevelGen.ts`, `src/lib/`, `src/components/`, game file sizes.

---

## 0. One honest framing before the scores

No proposal — and no synthesis of them — produces a game that beats Super Bear Adventure or Minecraft on raw fun. A 12×18 block grid is not Minecraft; emoji drawn with `fillText` are not a Toca Boca sprite set; eight engineers in one session do not ship a studio's art budget. The synthesizer should not promise the father that outcome.

What *can* be won, and what the proposals collectively make believable, is a different contest: the app becomes **his** (his photo, a bear he named, his father's voice and his father seeing his builds), it lives on the home screen, it feels like a real app (crisp, fast, no accidental exits, speaks to him), it has a collection and a world that grows, and it ends gracefully instead of being torn away. That is the realistic win condition: *he opens it willingly most days for 20–30 minutes, learns while doing it, and does not fight the stop.* Every score below is calibrated against that, not against "replaces Minecraft".

### 1.1 game-designer — 8 / 7 / 5 / 7 = 27

**As the child.** This is the only proposal whose first 60 seconds I would stay for. Tap my face → a bear talks → a map with a bear island on it → Süper Ayı is open in a second with "Hazır? Üç, iki, bir!" and the buttons are under my thumbs. Nothing to read. Then stars fly to the top bar, a sticker peels off, and there is a construction site on the map I want to open. The two genuinely new *toys* — Blok Dünyası (persistent "my worlds", a bear walking on what I built, "Babaya göster") and Merak Akışı (swipe, one picture, one sentence, one tap, ends) — are aimed precisely at the two habits the father named. The sticker album with silhouettes for locked stickers is textbook collection pull, done without gambling. Day-7 reasons to return are real: the streak sun, Bal's hat at level 7, three saved worlds, today's picture quests.

Why not 9: the honey economy as written is **−1 honey per arcade run**. A six-year-old on Flappy Bird dies every 5–15 seconds. Six free honey is ninety seconds of Flappy, then a gate; even the doubled 12 for the first three days is three minutes. The proposal's own vision ("after the third run the bear says 'Balım bitti'") shows the wall arriving inside the first five minutes of day one. That is exactly the moment he goes back to Super Bear. The wellbeing proposal's fix (drain by *time*, never per run) rescues the mechanic; with that fix this is a 9.

**Learning (7).** F8 is a real engine (generators, 3 levels, MEB sound-group order, Hayat Bilgisi safety content, fixes for the fake exercises). But its mastery model is thin (streak-3 up / miss-2 down, no spaced review, no prerequisite graph), and the Bal Kapısı turns practice into a toll booth — "the honest version of watch-an-ad" is still an interstitial in front of the thing he wants, and the research on extrinsic gates crowding out intrinsic motivation is not nothing. It also did not catch the phoneme problem (Turkish TTS says letter *names*, not sounds) that pedagogy caught.

**Feasibility (5).** This is where it breaks. Count the Phase 1 deliverables: a new sandbox game with a pure engine, atlas renderer, five validators, 15 seeded challenges, PNG export to Firebase; 16 generators × 3 levels; four 12-card episodes; 10 game migrations plus all 14 lint errors; 120 flat SVG stickers; a 6-frame bear atlas with 20 cosmetic overlays; six island illustrations; a 24-sound earcon palette; PWA, error boundaries, CI, tests, parent panel v2, settings. E8 "Art, audio & copy" alone (120 stickers + atlas + 6 illustrations + rewriting 69 guide intros) is more than one agent-session. The shared-contract and integration-order thinking is good, but the plan has no cut list; it is a Phase 1 + Phase 2 roadmap labelled Phase 1.

**Wellbeing (7).** Deterministic finite rewards, no timers-you-can-pay-to-skip, streak drops by one instead of resetting, Baba ile behind a gate, "Bal uyuyor" time budget, no external links. Good instincts. Missing: no break design, no evening wind-down, no grace period, and the per-run honey is itself a wellbeing problem (it punishes failing fast, which is what six-year-olds do).

### 1.2 pedagogy — 6 / 10 / 6 / 7 = 29

**As the child.** The individual exercises are the best-designed learning moments in any proposal: "li-mon… hangi harf?" with the picture pulsing and the voice stretching "lll-imon" on a miss; ducks in two rows of five; the heart beating on screen with "zıpla, sonra elini göğsüne koy". These are game-feel, not worksheet-feel. The Phase 2 idea of honey pots in Süper Ayı carrying the letters he is learning, with the bear *shouting the sound* on pickup, is the single best "learning inside the fun half" idea on the panel.

But the day-1 flow is wrong for *this* child. The map opens with one glowing tile and the Oyun Parkı island carries a **lock icon "until quest done or energy > 0"**, and the vision has him complete three learning tasks before "Oyun Parkı açıldı". The father has already lost this exact battle ("I failed to interest him"); the first thing the new app does must not be "three tasks, then your game". From the child's seat that is homework with a bear sticker on it, and I would tap the home button inside the first minute. No sandbox, no companion character, no new arcade content, and the arcade retune is mostly Phase 2 — so the fun half of the app is the current fun half with better buttons. Engagement 6, and it would be 8 if the opening were the wellbeing proposal's (nothing locked for three days, Süper Ayı as hero tile) with the same quest tile glowing beside it.

**Learning (10).** Nothing else is close. A 34-skill graph with MEB codes and prerequisite edges, so the parent panel speaks the teacher's language and the quest picker is automatic. Mastery with explicit, explainable rules plus a Leitner box for spaced review (the "yesterday's letter comes back" effect that produces school transfer). Scaffolded retry before reveal, interleaving, 6-item sets. The phoneme insight — never ask TTS to pronounce a bare consonant; always word + picture; the father's recorded voice as the Phase 2 fix — is the one piece of domain knowledge in the whole panel that would otherwise have produced a subtly broken product. Hayat Bilgisi as a third of 1. sınıf, with `hb.guvenli` including "ekranda korkunç bir şey gördün → kapat, büyüğüne göster" — the only place the father's protection goal is *taught* rather than enforced. Executive-function games (Dur-Git go/no-go) chosen for what a game-habituated child most needs. Process praise. The 69-slug keep/rebuild/merge/remove table is the most useful content audit on the panel.

**Feasibility (6).** More disciplined than game-designer (no sandbox, no 120 stickers, no atlas; "ship 5 levels but only L1–L3 need content"), and it names what is *not* in Phase 1. Still heavy: 34 skills, six renderers including a TraceCanvas with 58 letter stroke paths plus digits, ~12 generators with a ≥12-picture-words-per-sound word bank, 52 capsules, Dur-Git, plus shell, game infra and production. SortBins drag-and-drop on mobile Safari is a known rabbit hole the author flags themselves. Achievable if generators are cut to ~8 and letter paths to groups 1–2.

**Wellbeing (7).** Energy tank only refilled by learning, soft bedtime, Mola that offers a capsule not more arcade, weekly streak freeze, praise rules, adult text only under parent mode. Missing breaks, grace period, predicted countdown; and the lock-until-quest opening is itself a wellbeing risk (it manufactures the conflict).

### 1.3 mobile-production — 6 / 3 / 9 / 6 = 24

**As the child.** What I would notice: the app is on my home screen with a bear icon, it opens full-screen without a browser bar and without a white flash, Süper Ayı is crisp and runs at the right speed, the buttons are where my thumbs are, the phone buzzes when I grab a coin, and I cannot fall out of the game by hitting a link. When I die, a two-second card *tells* me "2 yıldız!" instead of showing three lines about my father's score. That is the difference between "a website Dad shows me" and "my game", and the father's own diagnosis ("can't compete with Super Bear") is half about exactly this feel. But there is no character, no new toy, no sandbox, no capsule content, and the learning half is still `QuizGame` with a `slug` prop. Islands and stickers exist as architecture; the content that fills them is someone else's job. A strong foundation that is not, by itself, a reason to open the app tomorrow.

**Learning (3).** Deliberately out of scope; `speech.ts`, the progress store's `skills` slice and the `QuizGame` hook are seams for others.

**Feasibility (9).** The only plan I believe will be green at the end of the session. Every Next 16 claim carries a doc path and several were verified against source (`discover.js:71` for basePath on the manifest link; `apple-icon` PNG-only; `headers()` unsupported under export so `updateViaCache: "none"`). Concrete `manifest.ts`. A hand-written service worker with a strategy table and a reason for not using Serwist (Turbopack). The exact 14 lint fixes by rule. `<Activity/>` instead of `{Activity()}` — a latent rules-of-hooks bug that also blocks the per-game error boundary. Playwright against the preinstalled chromium with no download. Frozen contracts, an integration order, and an explicit "not in Phase 1" list. The performance budget (820 KB → 250 KB on `/`) is enforced by a script, not a hope.

**Wellbeing (6).** PIN with lockout, zero child-reachable external links as a post-build test, Firebase rules + anonymous auth, error logging to the parent, no Web Push by principle. But time budget and Mola are Phase 2, and there is no praise, break, or bedtime design.

### 1.4 parent-wellbeing — 6 / 5 / 8 / 10 = 29

**As the child.** This proposal produces no new fun, but it is the one that keeps the fun from being taken away badly — which is what actually ends these experiments. "Rekorun hâlâ burada: 1240" voiced on day one is the right first sentence: my game is still my game. Naming the bear from four voiced chips is cheap ownership. Three days with nothing gated. Every end screen has two *equal* buttons, so stopping is a choice I make, not a wall I hit. The cap is announced at −5 and −1 and lets me finish the run; the end screen shows tomorrow's preview ("Yarın Sayı Dağı'nda yeni bir şey var") so tomorrow has a reason before today ends. The amber evening map and the sleeping bear are how bedtime already works at home with lights.

The one thing I would resent: a **mandatory, unskippable 60-second ring every 20 minutes**. Unskippable interstitials are the thing children learn to hate about apps; a six-year-old mid-flow in Tetris will not experience "Flamingo ol!" as a game the first few times. The first-break-with-father mitigation is right; the default should still be gentler (25 min, 30–45 s ring, parent-tunable).

**Learning (5).** Indirect: `praise.ts` with a banned-word test is real pedagogy (growth mindset, no person praise, no comparison with the father), the break cards carry ritmik sayma and body awareness, and co-play behind an arithmetic gate is the strongest protective factor at this age. No content of its own.

**Feasibility (8).** Almost entirely pure functions with a test list already written (`computePhase` matrix, `honeyLeft`, `praise` no-repeat/banned-words/≤7 words, breaks table, capsules shape). Two engineers. Edge cases that would otherwise bite in week one are already thought through (phone lock mid-run, midnight rollover, two tabs, offline outbox). It depends on sibling integration points, but names each one.

**Wellbeing (10).** Complete and decisive. It makes the one decision the other proposals left open (one meter, time-drained, learning fills it, stars not honey buy cosmetics). Designed stopping points in every loop. Predicted cap that never cuts a run. Breaks. Evening mode and bedtime with a wake time. Co-play with an adult gate. Praise through one module. A dashboard that answers "how much, what, is it working, what do we do tonight" in 20 seconds. A transition plan that protects Süper Ayı's identity and high score. Zero external surface as a CI test. An explicit anti-pattern list. This is the layer that turns "an app he likes" into "an app his father can let him have".

## 3. Ideas to REJECT (or defer / modify), with reasons

1. **Honey −1 per arcade run** [game-designer F4/F5]. Punishes dying fast, which is what six-year-olds do; the gate arrives inside the first five minutes of day one by the proposal's own vision. Replace with time-based drain (wellbeing F1).
2. **Oyun Parkı locked until Günün Görevi is done** [pedagogy F6 and 2.5 vision]. "Three tasks, then your game" is the framing the father has already lost with. Quests are a glowing suggestion that fills the jar; the arcade is open from minute one with the base jar.
3. **Bal Kapısı as a forced interstitial over the paused game** [game-designer F5]. Keep the *idea* (three voiced picture items at the moment of motivation), reject the *form*. With a time-based jar the natural place is the `RewardSheet` when the jar is empty: "Bal bitti. 3 soru → +2 bal" as one of two or three equal choices, never a sheet that slides up over the game you were about to restart. Also strips the "watch an ad to continue" smell.
4. **120 hand-drawn SVG stickers, 20 cosmetic overlays, a 6-frame bear atlas and six island illustrations in Phase 1** [game-designer F13 / E8]. Not deliverable in one session by engineers, and engineer-drawn art that misses reads worse than emoji on a well-designed card. Phase 1: ~30 stickers as emoji-on-card with named triggers, 5 cosmetics as SVG overlays, one bear sprite (the existing Süper Ayı one). Art is Phase 2.
5. **Blok Dünyası challenge mode, five validators, 15 seeded challenges and PNG export to a Firebase gallery in Phase 1** [game-designer F6]. Free build + 5 saves + bear walking + day/night slider is the week-1 value; the rest is Phase 2. Keep `engine.ts` pure so validators can land later.
6. **Four capsule episodes (48 cards) in Phase 1** [game-designer F7, pedagogy F5]. Two episodes done well (`isik-ve-golge`, which both proposals pick, and `kalbim-ve-nefesim`, pedagogy's strongest demo) beat four done at the old register. The quality bar is "he repeats it to his mother"; that takes rewriting time.
7. **16 generators × 3 levels, 58 letter stroke paths plus digits, `SortBins` drag-and-drop** [game-designer F8, pedagogy F2–F4]. Phase 1 content: `sesAyirt`, `sesHarf`, `kelime`, `sayma`, `toplama`, `cikarma`, `oruntu`, `guvenli` at L1–L3; letters for sound groups 1–2 only (12 lowercase); tap-to-select instead of drag (pedagogy flags the mobile-Safari drag risk itself).
8. **Unskippable 60-second break ring every 20 minutes as the Phase 1 default** [wellbeing F4]. Keep the break cards and the bear; default 25 minutes, 30–45 s ring, parent-tunable, first break always co-play. An unskippable minute is the one moment I, as the child, would hold against the app.
9. **Clock-tampering detection that forces night mode until a parent confirms** [wellbeing Appendix B.3]. False positives (DST, travel, a parent fixing the clock) lock the child out and need the father. Phase 1: log `clock_jump` to the Sağlık card only.
10. **Landscape layer with `requestFullscreen` + `screen.orientation.lock` and rotate prompts** [mobile-production F4]. Every wide game is behind Baba ile; nothing on the child's default path needs it. Phase 2.
11. **Playwright e2e as a blocking CI gate in Phase 1** [mobile-production F13/F14]. Valuable, but a flaky e2e in a one-session CI costs the integration hour. Write the config and one smoke, run it non-blocking; Vitest + `verify-out` are the gate.
12. **Double parent gate (arithmetic + PIN) in front of the login's Baba card** [game-designer F1]. One gate per purpose: PIN for `/bilgi`, arithmetic for co-play presence (wellbeing F6).
13. **Rivalry with the father visible to the child** (current `ScoreHud`, `checkLiveMilestone` messages). All four agree: parent-only, `rivalVisible` default off. A six-year-old losing to his father's high score every day is motivating for a week and discouraging after.
14. **`/kesfet` long articles, source links, `ActivityKnowledgePanel`, `IntroModal` text walls on the child surface.** All four agree: parent reference only; `IntroModal` → one picture + one voiced sentence + giant Başla.
15. **Web Push, badge counts, streak reset to zero, loot-box randomness, autoplay chains, any shop with pseudo-money.** [wellbeing anti-pattern list; mobile-production notes push is impossible under export anyway]. Never.
16. **`dovus-arenasi` on the child's map.** [pedagogy, wellbeing]. Parent-mode only; a 1,077-line sparring game is the wrong flagship for "zararlı içerikten korunsun".

## 4. CONFLICTS the synthesizer must resolve (with my resolution)

1. **Honey (per run) vs energy (per minute, learning-only refill) vs jar (time drain, learning fills, stars for cosmetics).** [game-designer F4 / pedagogy 2.5 / wellbeing F1] → **Wellbeing's jar.** One meter; 1 cell = 2 min; drains only while an arcade game is running and visible; +2 cells per 6-item set, +1 per episode, ceiling `arcadeMaxMinutes`; stars buy cosmetics; Blok free build and capsules never drain the jar but count toward the daily total. `progress.honey` is *derived* from `wellbeing.honeyLeft()`, not a separate counter.
2. **Day-1 opening: arcade locked behind the quest (pedagogy) vs gated after six runs (game-designer) vs three-day grace with Süper Ayı as hero (wellbeing).** → **Wellbeing's.** Grace until install day + 2; quests glow, never gate; "Devam et ▶ Süper Ayı — Rekorun hâlâ burada". The bear explains the jar on day 3 in one sentence.
3. **Companion name: fixed "Bal" (game-designer) vs child names it (wellbeing).** → Child names it from four voiced chips, default "Bal"; `settings.companionName`; every string uses `{ayı}`. "Bal Kapısı" becomes the jar offer, not a named screen.
4. **Island set: six + hidden Baba ile (game-designer) vs Harf Adası / Sayı Dağı / Şekil Ormanı / Merak Gölü / Oyun Parkı / Babamla (pedagogy) vs seven derived from MENU (mobile-production).** → Game-designer's six (Macera Adası, Harf Ormanı, Sayı Dağı, Şekil Köyü, Merak Gölü, Blok Dünyası) + hidden Baba ile, because it gives the sandbox a place on the map; pedagogy's Günün Görevi is a glowing *tile* on the map, not an island; `worlds.ts` derived from a regrouped `MENU` as mobile-production specifies, so the build-time invariants keep holding.
5. **Learning engine ownership: game-designer F8 `Exercise` (simple mastery) vs pedagogy engine (skill graph, Leitner, 5 levels, six renderers).** → **Pedagogy's types and rules** (`skills.ts`, `mastery.ts`, `engine/types.ts`); they are a superset and MEB-coded. Game-designer's generator list maps onto pedagogy's skill ids. Content at L1–L3 only in Phase 1. Four renderers (`ChoiceCard`, `TapCount`, `OrderStrip`, `TraceCanvas`); `SortBins` and `MirrorGrid` Phase 2.
6. **Capsule route: `/merak/[topic]/` (game-designer) vs `/kesfet/[slug]/kapsul/` (pedagogy).** → `/merak/[topic]/` with `generateStaticParams` over topics that have episodes; `/kesfet/*` stays as the parent reference and is not linked from the child surface. Shared `src/lib/capsules.ts` type. Two episodes in Phase 1 (see Reject 6).
7. **Persistence: event-sourced reducer store with merge laws (mobile-production F11) vs flat `progress.ts` state (game-designer F4) vs `settings.ts` + `sessionClock` day buckets (wellbeing).** → **Mobile-production's store is the single persistence layer** (`dogukan-progress-v1:{player}`, pure reducers, `useProgress(selector)`, monotonic-max merge). Game-designer's rules (star table, streak, quests, stickers) are reducers on it; wellbeing's `DayUsage` is a slice of it written by `sessionClock`; `Settings` is a separate key as wellbeing proposes. Key separator is `:` (three proposals) not `-<player>` (game-designer).
8. **The word "Mola": a 20-minute break (wellbeing F4) vs the daily-cap screen (pedagogy 2.5, mobile-production F17).** → `MolaScreen` = break, `LimitScreen` = daily cap, `NightScreen` = bedtime. Breaks included in Phase 1 with the softened defaults in Reject 8.

## 5. Closing note for the synthesizer

The four proposals are unusually complementary: game-designer supplies the *world and the toys*, pedagogy supplies *what is actually learned and how*, mobile-production supplies *the only credible path to a green build*, and wellbeing supplies *the rules that stop the experiment from dying on day four*. The synthesis should take each of them for exactly that and not try to average them. The two decisions that matter most for whether Doğukan is still opening this app in week two are: (a) nothing he loves is locked on day one and the jar is drained by time, not by dying; (b) every string he meets is spoken and short. Everything else is polish on top of those two.
